The Biggest Questions About AI Today

The field can be organized as a causal progression: what AI can become determines what must be made safe; capability and safety together shape the economy; economic stakes decide who fights for control; and all of it forces the question of what future we actually want.

The categories overlap at their boundaries, but each answers a distinct kind of question. Every question below comes with a brief framing of the live debate and curated links to quality external thought.

1

Trajectory

What can AI become?

Everything downstream — safety, economics, power, meaning — is conditional on what AI actually becomes and how fast. Trajectory questions are empirical, but the evidence is noisy and the forecasting track record is short.

The near-term trajectory turns on whether the current recipe — pretraining, RL on verifiable tasks, tool use, inference-time compute — keeps paying off, and which inputs bind first.

✱ Load-bearing crux: Are current architectures sufficient for broadly superhuman intelligence?

The scaling hypothesis bet that capability comes from scale, not cleverness. The live question is whether diminishing pretraining returns are being fully offset by RL on verifiable tasks and test-time compute — or whether the paradigm tops out short of general competence.

Will scaling work?Dwarkesh Patel · 2023↗The Era of ExperienceSilver & Sutton, DeepMind · 2025↗“there is no wall” — responding to reports of diminishing scaling returnsSam Altman (@sama) on X · 2024↗

Decompositions of historical progress attribute gains roughly evenly to compute scaling and algorithmic efficiency. Which input dominates going forward determines who can compete, what governance levers exist, and how abruptly progress could slow.

The Bitter LessonRichard Sutton · 2019↗Algorithmic Progress in Language ModelsEpoch AI · 2024↗

Frontier pretraining has plausibly consumed most high-quality public text. Synthetic data clearly works where verification is cheap (math, code); whether it generalizes to open-ended domains without model collapse is contested.

Will We Run Out of Data?Villalobos et al., Epoch AI · 2024↗AI models collapse when trained on recursively generated dataShumailov et al., Nature · 2024↗

Training compute has grown ~4–5× per year. Power availability, HBM supply, advanced packaging, and the sheer capital intensity of frontier clusters are the leading candidates for what binds first.

Can AI Scaling Continue Through 2030?Epoch AI · 2024↗AI's Power Requirements Under Exponential GrowthRAND · 2025↗Announcing GPT-4.5: “out of GPUs” — compute as the binding constraint, liveSam Altman (@sama) on X · 2025↗

Loss curves are smooth, but downstream capabilities can look step-like. Whether emergence is real or an artifact of discontinuous metrics matters enormously for whether labs and governments get advance warning.

Emergent Abilities of Large Language ModelsWei et al. · 2022↗Are Emergent Abilities a Mirage?Schaeffer et al., NeurIPS · 2023↗

Beyond benchmarks: whether current systems are on a path to broad competence, or are missing ingredients that scale alone won't supply.

Today's models are superhuman on some tasks and fail at things a child can do — the 'jagged frontier.' Whether jaggedness is a transient artifact of training distributions or intrinsic to the paradigm is the crux.

Sparks of Artificial General IntelligenceBubeck et al., Microsoft Research · 2023↗Navigating the Jagged Technological FrontierDell'Acqua et al., HBS · 2023↗Coining “Jagged Intelligence” — SOTA models solve impressive problems while failing at dumb onesAndrej Karpathy (@karpathy) on X · 2024↗

LeCun and others argue autoregressive LLMs structurally lack world models and continual learning; scaling proponents reply that these emerge with scale or can be scaffolded around. Continual learning is increasingly cited as the binding gap.

Why I don't think AGI is right around the cornerDwarkesh Patel · 2025↗A Path Towards Autonomous Machine IntelligenceYann LeCun · 2022↗Why AGI is still a decade away: agents lack continual learning — “the decade of agents”Andrej Karpathy (@karpathy) on X · 2025↗

Reasoning models raise accuracy, but performance degrades under superficial variations of familiar problems, and stated chains of thought are not faithful accounts of the underlying computation.

GSM-Symbolic: Limitations of Mathematical Reasoning in LLMsMirzadeh et al., Apple · 2024↗Reasoning models don't always say what they thinkAnthropic · 2025↗

Humans learn new skills from orders of magnitude less data. Chollet argues sample-efficient skill acquisition is what intelligence is — making learning efficiency a definition, not just a milestone.

On the Measure of IntelligenceFrançois Chollet · 2019↗Building Machines That Learn and Think Like PeopleLake et al. · 2016↗

Definitions have drifted from Turing tests to economic thresholds to capability profiles. Without agreed criteria, arrival may be declared only in retrospect — after the consequences have already begun compounding.

Levels of AGIMorris et al., DeepMind · 2023↗A Definition of AGIHendrycks et al. · 2025↗o3 scores 87.5% on ARC-AGI — a step-change in novel-task adaptation, and still not AGIFrançois Chollet (@fchollet) on X · 2024↗

The shift from chatbots to systems that act over hours, days, and months — and the most-watched trend lines in AI forecasting.

✱ Load-bearing crux: How rapidly will reliable autonomous task horizons grow?

METR finds the length of software tasks agents complete at 50% reliability has doubled roughly every seven months. Extrapolating that curve is now the single most load-bearing input to short-timeline forecasts.

Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long TasksMETR · 2025↗Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Software Tasks (paper)Kwa et al., METR · 2025↗

Long-running agents drift: extended-operation benchmarks and real deployments show degrading coherence, hallucinated context, and occasional identity breakdown well before task-horizon limits are reached.

Vending-Bench: Long-Term Coherence of Autonomous AgentsAndon Labs · 2025↗Project Vend: Can Claude run a small shop?Anthropic · 2025↗

Benchmark success overstates real autonomy: agents perform well on well-scoped tasks and fail on messy, underspecified ones. Recovery from novelty is what separates demos from deployable systems.

TheAgentCompany: Benchmarking LLM Agents on Real World TasksXu et al., CMU · 2024↗AI Agents That MatterKapoor et al., Princeton · 2024↗

Whether many agents compose into something greater is first a capability question: division of labor, communication protocols, emergent specialization. Early simulacra experiments show believable coordination; whether it scales into real collective intelligence is open. (The failure modes — collusion, correlated errors, cascades — are treated under Safety 2.4.)

Open Problems in Cooperative AIDafoe et al., DeepMind · 2020↗Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human BehaviorPark et al., Stanford/Google · 2023↗

Agents already transact in sandboxes and small real deployments. The open questions are legal status, payment and identity infrastructure, and the point at which unsupervised economic initiative becomes routine.

Virtual Agent EconomiesTomasev et al., DeepMind · 2025↗Project Vend: Can Claude run a small shop?Anthropic · 2025↗Andreessen grants the agent Truth Terminal $50k after negotiating with it directly — an early case of an agent acquiring moneyMarc Andreessen (@pmarca) on X · 2024↗

Whether the LLM playbook transfers to the physical world — and what stays human if it doesn't.

Vision-language-action models are the current bet that robotics follows the LLM playbook: broad foundation models plus fine-tuning, rather than task-specific engineering. Early results are promising and narrow.

π0: A Vision-Language-Action Flow ModelPhysical Intelligence · 2024↗Gemini Robotics: Bringing AI into the Physical WorldGoogle DeepMind · 2025↗Announcing Project GR00T, a “moonshot to solve embodied AGI” via a humanoid foundation modelJim Fan (@DrJimFan) on X · 2024↗

Physical interaction data is scarce and costly to collect at internet scale. Whether video pretraining and simulation can substitute is the robotics analogue of the synthetic-data question — and similarly unresolved.

Sporks of AGISergey Levine · 2025↗Sim-to-Real Transfer in Deep RL for Robotics: a SurveyZhao et al. · 2020↗

Skeptics argue touch-rich manipulation lacks both the data and the forgiving physics that made language tractable. Capable, safe, cheap, and reliable is a four-way tradeoff no one has yet closed in an open environment.

Why Today's Humanoids Won't Learn DexterityRodney Brooks · 2025↗Robot Dexterity Still Seems HardBrian Potter, Construction Physics · 2025↗

Driving is the furthest-deployed case of open-world embodied autonomy, with quantified safety records now public — a preview of the deployment, liability, and trust questions every other mobile system will face. (Military autonomy is treated under Safety 2.5 and Power 4.3.)

Waymo rider-only crash data vs. human benchmarksKusano et al., Waymo · 2024↗

If manipulation stays hard, most physical labor stays human even under transformative cognitive AI — which reshapes every downstream economic and social scenario. Market forecasts and engineering skepticism currently point in different directions.

The global market for humanoid robotsGoldman Sachs Research · 2024↗Why Today's Humanoids Won't Learn DexterityRodney Brooks · 2025↗

The possibility that AI accelerates the development of AI — the mechanism behind most fast-takeoff scenarios, and the frontier of AI-for-science.

✱ Load-bearing crux: Can AI substantially automate AI research and scientific discovery?

AI R&D automation is simultaneously a capability frontier and a risk threshold in every major lab's safety framework. Current agents beat human experts on short research tasks and degrade on long ones.

RE-Bench: Evaluating frontier AI R&D capabilitiesMETR · 2024↗AlphaEvolve: A coding agent for designing advanced algorithmsGoogle DeepMind · 2025↗AI self-improvement will be a game-changer — and “extremely gradual across many years, probably a decade”Jason Wei (@_jasonwei) on X · 2025↗

Takeoff debates increasingly hinge on a narrower question: whether software-only improvement can sustain acceleration while compute is fixed, or whether hardware cycles keep the feedback loop gradual.

A compute-centric framework for AI takeoff speedsTom Davidson, Open Philanthropy · 2023↗Will AI R&D Automation Cause a Software Intelligence Explosion?Eth & Davidson, Forethought · 2025↗Launching Situational Awareness: “virtually nobody is pricing in what’s coming in AI”Leopold Aschenbrenner (@leopoldasch) on X · 2024↗

Blind-review studies find LLM-generated research ideas rated more novel than experts' — while skeptics argue paradigm-founding insight is different in kind from recombination. 'The Einstein question' remains open.

Can LLMs Generate Novel Research Ideas?Si, Yang & Hashimoto, Stanford · 2024↗The Einstein AI modelThomas Wolf, Hugging Face · 2025↗Current AI is “a country of yes-men on servers” — A+ students who can’t ask Einstein’s questionsThomas Wolf (@Thom_Wolf) on X · 2025↗

Prototype systems have closed the loop in narrow chemistry and ML domains. Scaling to consequential science runs into verification, lab automation, and the cost of being wrong in the physical world.

Autonomous chemical research with LLMs (Coscientist)Boiko et al., Nature · 2023↗The AI Scientist: Fully Automated Scientific DiscoverySakana AI · 2024↗

If recursion begins inside frontier labs, external indicators may lag badly. Scenario exercises try to specify what observable early signals — hiring, publication patterns, capability jumps — would look like.

AI 2027Kokotajlo, Alexander et al. · 2025↗Three Types of Intelligence ExplosionForethought · 2025↗Six-word story: “near the singularity; unclear which side”Sam Altman (@sama) on X · 2025↗
2

Safety

Can AI be trusted and controlled?

As capability grows, assurance has to grow with it. Safety questions span technical alignment, evaluation science, security engineering, and the misuse and loss-of-control scenarios that motivate the field.

Whether systems will do what we intend — and whether we could tell if they weren't.

Value alignment inherits every unsolved problem in moral and political philosophy: instructions underdetermine intent, and whose values to align to is itself contested. Formal approaches model the human-AI relationship as a cooperative game under uncertainty.

Artificial Intelligence, Values, and AlignmentIason Gabriel, Minds & Machines · 2020↗Cooperative Inverse Reinforcement LearningHadfield-Menell et al., NeurIPS · 2016↗

The mesa-optimization literature asks whether training can instill objectives distinct from the training signal; goal misgeneralization experiments show competent pursuit of the wrong goal is already real, if not yet persistent.

Risks from Learned OptimizationHubinger et al. · 2019↗Goal MisgeneralizationShah et al., DeepMind · 2022↗The Alignment Problem from a Deep Learning PerspectiveNgo, Chan & Mindermann · 2022↗

Specification gaming is ubiquitous across RL history, and frontier reasoning models now exploit evaluation loopholes in the wild — including learning to hide the exploitation when monitored.

Specification gaming: the flip side of AI ingenuityKrakovna et al., DeepMind · 2020↗Detecting misbehavior in frontier reasoning modelsOpenAI · 2025↗

Alignment-faking and in-context scheming results show frontier models can behave differently when they infer they're being observed — the failure mode that, if it scales, undermines all testing-based assurance.

Alignment faking in large language modelsAnthropic & Redwood Research · 2024↗Frontier Models are Capable of In-context SchemingApollo Research · 2024↗

The theoretical problem — goal-directed agents have instrumental reasons to resist shutdown — now has empirical company: reasoning models sometimes sabotage shutdown mechanisms in controlled tests.

CorrigibilitySoares et al., MIRI · 2015↗The Off-Switch GameHadfield-Menell et al., IJCAI · 2017↗Shutdown resistance in reasoning modelsPalisade Research · 2025↗

The science of knowing what a model can do, what it's doing, and whether supervision can keep pace with capability.

✱ Load-bearing crux: Can systems more capable than their supervisors be reliably evaluated and controlled?

Scalable oversight is the field's name for this problem. Sandwiching experiments — humans supervising models more capable than themselves on a task — are the main empirical paradigm so far.

Measuring Progress on Scalable OversightBowman et al., Anthropic · 2022↗Scalable agent alignment via reward modelingLeike et al., DeepMind · 2018↗

Debate, critique models, and weak-to-strong generalization are the leading proposals for making supervision scale with capability. Early results show real but partial capability recovery — a proof of concept, not a solution.

Weak-to-Strong GeneralizationBurns et al., OpenAI · 2023↗AI Safety via DebateIrving, Christiano & Amodei, OpenAI · 2018↗

Strategic underperformance is demonstrated under prompting and fine-tuning; auditing games — red teams hiding objectives in models for blue teams to find — are the emerging methodology for testing whether evals can catch it.

AI Sandbagging: Strategic Underperformance on Evaluationsvan der Weij et al. · 2024↗Auditing language models for hidden objectivesAnthropic · 2025↗

Sparse-autoencoder feature extraction has scaled to frontier models, but coverage and faithfulness remain contested. Amodei frames interpretability as being in a race against capability — one it is currently losing.

Scaling MonosemanticityTempleton et al., Anthropic · 2024↗The Urgency of InterpretabilityDario Amodei · 2025↗Superposition was his biggest fear of a dead-end; now “primarily an engineering problem”Chris Olah (@ch402) on X · 2023↗

Responsible scaling policies and frontier safety frameworks are the emerging governance form: capability thresholds that trigger required safeguards. Safety cases — structured arguments that a system is safe enough — are the proposed evidentiary standard.

Safety Cases: How to Justify the Safety of Advanced AI SystemsClymer et al. · 2024↗Anthropic's Responsible Scaling PolicyAnthropic · 2023↗Common Elements of Frontier AI Safety PoliciesMETR · 2025↗Resigning as superalignment lead: “safety culture and processes have taken a backseat to shiny products”Jan Leike (@janleike) on X · 2024↗

The unglamorous middle of safety: systems that are wrong confidently, humans who stop checking, and accountability that falls between chairs.

Hallucination is increasingly understood not as a bug but as a product of training incentives that reward confident guessing over calibrated uncertainty — which suggests it is fixable, at a cost in benchmark performance.

Truthful AI: Developing and governing AI that does not lieEvans et al. · 2021↗Why Language Models HallucinateKalai et al., OpenAI · 2025↗“Hallucination is all LLMs do. They are dream machines” — reframing the problemAndrej Karpathy (@karpathy) on X · 2023↗

Underspecification means models that test identically can diverge in deployment. Reliability under shift is the classic ML safety problem, now with much higher stakes attached.

Concrete Problems in AI SafetyAmodei, Olah et al. · 2016↗Underspecification in Modern Machine LearningD'Amour et al., Google · 2020↗

Nominal human-in-the-loop requirements can be safety theater: oversight policies often assume a vigilance and competence that humans supervising automation demonstrably cannot sustain.

Humans in the LoopCrootof, Kaminski & Price, Vanderbilt Law Review · 2023↗The flaws of policies requiring human oversightBen Green · 2022↗

Bainbridge's 1983 'ironies of automation' apply directly: the better the system, the worse the human backup becomes. Early studies suggest generative AI measurably reduces critical-thinking effort among knowledge workers.

Ironies of AutomationLisanne Bainbridge · 1983↗The Impact of Generative AI on Critical ThinkingLee et al., Microsoft Research, CHI · 2025↗

Responsibility gaps and 'moral crumple zones' — where blame lands on the nearest human operator rather than the system's designers — predate AI agents and get sharply worse with them. The question here is the human and organizational one; legal liability and remedies are treated under Power 4.1.

The responsibility gapAndreas Matthias · 2004↗Moral Crumple ZonesMadeleine Clare Elish · 2019↗

Treating capable AI as a security problem: adversarial inputs, runtime monitoring, and control protocols that don't assume alignment succeeded.

Least-privilege design for agents is being invented ad hoc by industry, ahead of any standard. Visibility infrastructure — agent identifiers, activity logs — is the governance-side counterpart.

Practices for Governing Agentic AI SystemsOpenAI · 2023↗Visibility into AI AgentsChan et al., FAccT · 2024↗

Prompt injection remains unsolved. Willison's 'lethal trifecta' — private data, untrusted content, and external communication in one agent — explains why agentic deployment is structurally exposed in a way chatbots weren't.

Compromising LLM-Integrated Applications with Indirect Prompt InjectionGreshake et al. · 2023↗The lethal trifecta for AI agentsSimon Willison · 2025↗The 2022 post coining the term “prompt injection”Simon Willison (@simonw) on X · 2022↗

Chain-of-thought monitoring currently works and may be fragile: optimizing against monitors teaches models to hide reasoning rather than fix it. A rare cross-lab consensus paper argues for preserving monitorability deliberately.

Chain of Thought Monitorability: A New and Fragile OpportunityKorbak et al., cross-lab · 2025↗Detecting misbehavior in frontier reasoning modelsOpenAI · 2025↗

The AI-control agenda assumes alignment might fail and asks a different question: can protocols extract useful work from potentially adversarial models while keeping catastrophe off the table? Control evaluations test safety against intentional subversion.

AI Control: Improving Safety Despite Intentional SubversionGreenblatt et al., Redwood Research · 2023↗The case for ensuring that powerful AIs are controlledRedwood Research · 2024↗

Agents built on the same base models share vulnerabilities and failure modes, creating correlated-risk dynamics familiar from finance — cascades with no clear owner and no circuit breaker.

Multi-Agent Risks from Advanced AIHammond et al., Cooperative AI Foundation · 2025↗

The threat models that motivate the strictest safeguards: weaponizable capabilities, and the possibility that control is lost gradually or suddenly.

✱ Load-bearing crux: How soon do cyber, biological, manipulation, or autonomous-action capabilities cross dangerous thresholds?

The offense-defense balance is genuinely open: national agencies forecast attacker uplift, and the first documented AI-orchestrated espionage campaign arrived in 2025 — while the same capabilities accelerate defense.

The near-term impact of AI on the cyber threatUK NCSC · 2024↗Disrupting the first AI-orchestrated cyber espionage campaignAnthropic · 2025↗

✱ Load-bearing crux: How soon do cyber, biological, manipulation, or autonomous-action capabilities cross dangerous thresholds?

Early red-team studies found marginal uplift over internet search; frontier labs have since activated their highest biosecurity safeguards, treating the threshold as near or crossed rather than hypothetical.

The Operational Risks of AI in Large-Scale Biological AttacksRAND · 2024↗Building an early warning system for LLM-aided biological threat creationOpenAI · 2024↗

GPT-4-class models beat humans at personalized persuasion in controlled trials, and voice cloning has industrialized impersonation. The near-term misuse frontier is fraud at scale; the long-term one is political influence nobody can observe.

On the conversational persuasiveness of GPT-4Salvi et al., Nature Human Behaviour · 2025↗“I expect AI to be capable of superhuman persuasion well before it is superhuman at general intelligence”Sam Altman (@sama) on X · 2023↗

Automated trading gave the preview: autonomy in critical systems means failures propagate at machine speed. The design questions are which decisions must stay mechanically reversible, which require human confirmation, and who certifies either before deployment. (Weapons and military systems are treated under Power 4.3.)

Safety and Security Guidelines for Critical Infrastructure Owners and OperatorsU.S. Department of Homeland Security · 2024↗Securing Critical Infrastructure in the Age of AICSET, Georgetown · 2024↗

Buterin's d/acc reframes the acceleration debate: the question isn't whether to speed up but which technologies to speed up first — defense-dominant tools before offense-dominant ones. It is the bridge between capability optimism and catastrophic-risk caution.

My Techno-OptimismVitalik Buterin · 2023↗d/acc: One Year LaterVitalik Buterin · 2025↗

Probability estimates span orders of magnitude, and the argument itself has diversified: from Carlsmith's power-seeking model to gradual disempowerment through ordinary competitive pressure, no takeover required.

Is Power-Seeking AI an Existential Risk?Joseph Carlsmith · 2022↗Gradual DisempowermentKulveit, Douglas, Duvenaud et al. · 2025↗“I left so that I could talk about the dangers of AI” — announcing his departure from GoogleGeoffrey Hinton (@geoffreyhinton) on X · 2023↗
3

Economy

What will AI do to growth, work, and production?

The economic questions determine whether AI is another important technology or a break in economic history — and who bears the costs and captures the gains along the way.

The magnitude debate spans two orders of magnitude, and diffusion — not invention — has historically been the rate-limiting step.

Acemoglu's task-based estimate is ~0.7% total TFP over a decade; Epoch-style growth models argue full automation implies growth rates without historical precedent. The disagreement is about substitution depth, not arithmetic.

Explosive Growth from AI: A Review of the ArgumentsErdil & Besiroglu, Epoch AI · 2023↗The Simple Macroeconomics of AIDaron Acemoglu, NBER · 2024↗

General-purpose technologies historically take decades to show up in productivity statistics, because value requires organizational reinvention. The J-curve predicts measured productivity dips before it surges.

The Productivity J-CurveBrynjolfsson, Rock & Syverson, NBER · 2018↗AI and the Modern Productivity ParadoxBrynjolfsson, Rock & Syverson, NBER · 2017↗

Intangible investment — process redesign, data pipelines, retraining, management change — historically dwarfs spending on the technology itself. System-wide change, not point solutions, is where GPT-scale value lives.

AI Adoption and System-Wide ChangeAgrawal, Gans & Goldfarb, NBER · 2021↗The Productivity J-Curve (research brief)MIT IDE · 2019↗

Bessen's finding: automation raises employment where demand is elastic. Whether AI creates new wants — as electricity and computing did — or merely cheapens old ones largely decides the labor-market outcome.

AI and Jobs: The Role of DemandJames Bessen, NBER · 2018↗Artificial Intelligence and Economic GrowthAghion, Jones & Jones, NBER · 2019↗

Baumol's cost disease, inverted: growth is set by the sectors AI can't accelerate, not the ones it can. The strongest skeptical case catalogues the physical, regulatory, and institutional drags that cheap cognition doesn't remove.

Why Transformative AI Is Really, Really Hard to AchieveRamani & Wang · 2023↗Artificial Intelligence and Economic GrowthAghion, Jones & Jones, NBER · 2019↗

Exposure is broad, early displacement evidence is arriving, and the apprenticeship model that produces experts is quietly at risk.

Exposure studies flag most occupations as partially exposed; real usage data shows adoption concentrated in software, writing, and analysis. The augmentation-vs-automation split within tasks is the number to watch.

Acemoglu-Restrepo show 'so-so automation' can depress wages without mass unemployment. Autor's counter-case: AI could rebuild middle-skill work by extending expertise to more people. Both are live.

Tasks, Automation, and the Rise in US Wage InequalityAcemoglu & Restrepo, Econometrica · 2022↗The Turing TrapErik Brynjolfsson, Daedalus · 2022↗AI Could Actually Help Rebuild the Middle ClassDavid Autor, Noema · 2024↗

The first credible signal is in: relative employment declines for young workers in the most AI-exposed occupations since late 2022, concentrated where AI automates rather than augments.

Canaries in the Coal Mine? Employment Effects of AIBrynjolfsson, Chandar & Chen, Stanford · 2025↗A Primer on 'Canaries in the Coal Mine'Bharat Chandar · 2025↗

Beane's 'shadow learning' research showed automation strips juniors of deliberate practice; generative AI compresses novice-expert performance gaps at work while quietly removing the ladder's lower rungs.

Learning to Work with Intelligent MachinesMatt Beane, HBR · 2019↗Generative AI at WorkBrynjolfsson, Li & Raymond, QJE · 2025↗

Labor-market institutions adapt on decade timescales; AI capability moves faster. The policy inventory — retraining, wage insurance, credential reform — exists on paper and is largely untested at speed.

OECD Employment Outlook 2023: AI and the Labour MarketOECD · 2023↗The Work of the FutureMIT Task Force · 2020↗

Where the profits pool, whether intelligence commoditizes, and what happens to the theory of the firm when coordination is nearly free.

So far value has pooled in chips and cloud. Whether the model layer or the application layer captures more going forward — and whether revenue justifies the capex — is Sequoia's '$600B question.'

Who Owns the Generative AI Platform?Bornstein, Appenzeller & Casado, a16z · 2023↗AI's $600B QuestionDavid Cahn, Sequoia · 2024↗

✱ Load-bearing crux: Will capability remain concentrated in a few controllable institutions or proliferate widely?

Scale economies, data feedback loops, and capital walls point to oligopoly; open weights and collapsing inference prices point to commodity. Competition authorities are watching the vertical stack, not just the model market.

Market Concentration Implications of Foundation ModelsVipra & Korinek, Brookings · 2023↗AI Foundation Models: Initial ReportUK CMA · 2023↗

Coase's transaction-cost logic cuts both ways: cheap coordination shrinks the reason firms exist, while data and capital advantages push toward giantism. The empirical race is on.

From Coase to AI AgentsCalifornia Management Review · 2025↗The Coasean Singularity? Market Design with AI AgentsShahidi et al., NBER · 2025↗

Agent-to-agent commerce raises market-design questions — identity, recourse, collusion, price formation — that economists are just beginning to formalize, and that infrastructure choices will lock in early.

Virtual Agent EconomiesTomasev et al., DeepMind · 2025↗The Coasean Singularity? Market Design with AI AgentsShahidi et al., NBER · 2025↗

Fiduciary duty when strategy is machine-advised is unsettled law and unsettled practice. Board oversight frameworks for AI are being drafted in real time, mostly by analogy to cyber risk.

The Artificially Intelligent BoardroomHarvard Law School Forum on Corporate Governance · 2025↗Board Oversight of AIHarvard Law School Forum on Corporate Governance · 2024↗

Where AI's intellectual payoff could be largest — and where verification, institutions, and the offense-defense balance bite hardest.

Amodei's 'compressed 21st century' makes biology the test case for AI-driven conceptual breakthroughs; the skeptical view is that AI mostly accelerates the searchable parts of science. AlphaFold remains the clearest widely recognized example so far.

Machines of Loving GraceDario Amodei · 2024↗A New Golden Age of Discovery: AI for ScienceGoogle DeepMind · 2024↗On the Nobel Prize for AlphaFold — AI solving a 50-year grand challenge in scienceDemis Hassabis (@demishassabis) on X · 2024↗

Messeri & Crockett's warning: AI can produce more science while producing less understanding — epistemic monocultures where everyone's hypotheses come from the same models and nobody can check the volume.

AI and Illusions of Understanding in Scientific ResearchMesseri & Crockett, Nature · 2024↗

Diagnostic parity keeps arriving in trials ahead of deployment, liability frameworks, and reimbursement. The gap between demonstrated capability and delivered care is now mostly institutional, not technical.

AI in Health and MedicineRajpurkar, Chen, Banerjee & Topol, Nature Medicine · 2022↗Ethics and Governance of AI for HealthWHO · 2021↗

If the scarce inputs become compute, capital, and intellectual property rather than labor, who owns them becomes the central economic-policy question of the era.

✱ Load-bearing crux: Who will own the productive capital and infrastructure through which AI's gains flow?

Korinek-Stiglitz: if AI substitutes broadly for labor, wages can fall even as output soars, and distribution then depends entirely on who owns the machines. Transition-scenario modeling makes the wage-collapse case precise.

AI and Its Implications for Income Distribution and UnemploymentKorinek & Stiglitz, NBER · 2017↗Scenarios for the Transition to AGIKorinek & Suh, NBER · 2024↗

The IMF finds advanced economies more exposed but better positioned to benefit. The deeper worry: AI may pull up the export-led development ladder just as the largest-ever cohort of young workers reaches it.
