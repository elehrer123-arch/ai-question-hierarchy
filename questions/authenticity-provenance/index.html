Authenticity

How will people establish whether a video, voice, document, identity, or event is genuine?

Chesney-Citron's 'liar's dividend' identified the deeper harm early: not that fakes are believed, but that real evidence becomes deniable. Provenance standards like C2PA are the infrastructure response.

What changed
November 2025–August 2026 · swept August 3, 2026 · editorial review pending
01

Provenance infrastructure took a technical hit just as the need for it peaked: the first formal-methods audit of C2PA found its core protocols fail their own security goals, while WITNESS argued that post-Sora, detection is essential but insufficient. The courts became the sharpest test case — fabricated audio and video are reaching courtrooms faster than detection can cope, and the federal evidence-rules committee left its AI amendments 'in the bullpen.' The liar's dividend is now measured, not just theorized: falsely crying 'deepfake' outperforms apologizing.
