1.4.4

Autonomous mobility

How capable will self-driving vehicles, drones, ships, and industrial machines become outside controlled settings?

Driving is the furthest-deployed case of open-world embodied autonomy, with quantified safety records now public — a preview of the deployment, liability, and trust questions every other mobile system will face. (Military autonomy is treated under Safety 2.5 and Power 4.3.)

What changed · November 2025–August 2026 · latest sweep August 3, 2026 · editorial review pending
01

Independent validation arrived: IIHS — not Waymo — finds 68% fewer police-reportable crashes than human drivers over 50 million driverless miles, while Waymo's own 170-million-mile update claims a serious-injury crash prevented every eight days. The number that changes the economics debate: roughly 70 remote overseers per 3,000 vehicles. Tesla's robotaxi data, by contrast, has to be reverse-engineered — and looks an order of magnitude behind.

Insurance Institute for Highway Safety · IIHS · 2026-07 report
Waymo's driverless cars crash less often than people
The results show that, on a limited scale, these driverless cars are safer than human drivers — who can be impaired or drowsy or suffer lapses in attention.

First fully independent validation of robotaxi safety claims: 68% lower police-reportable crash rates over 50 million driverless miles, with caveats on small samples in Austin and inadequate federal data collection.

Waymo · Waymo · 2026-03 report
Waymo Safety Impact update: 170 million rider-only miles
our analysis suggests the Waymo Driver is preventing approximately one serious-injury crash every 8 days.

Update to the canonical rider-only crash benchmark: 92% fewer serious-or-fatal-injury crashes than matched human drivers across 170M autonomous miles — the largest self-reported safety dataset in open-world autonomy.
