4.5.2

Concentration of power

Is it acceptable for a small number of private companies to steward systems with potentially society-wide influence?

The concern now runs beyond market power: analyses of AI-enabled coups argue that whoever controls advanced AI could convert it into political power directly — making internal lab governance a constitutional question.

What changed · October 2025–August 2026 · latest sweep August 3, 2026 · editorial review pending
01

A new axis opened: after Washington forced frontier models off the market, the contest is labs versus the executive branch, not just labs versus everyone else. Cotra argues a company could rival states outright; the OpenAI-restructuring advocates moved to SEC disclosure; a funder now treats extreme concentration as a research field. The discomfort went mainstream: Krugman argues AI's shock is landing on an already-oligarchic wealth distribution, and Amodei conceded on the record being 'somewhat uncomfortable' with a concentration of power arriving 'almost overnight, almost by accident.'
