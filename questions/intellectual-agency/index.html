Intellectual agency

How can people preserve curiosity, memory, judgment, writing ability, and the capacity to formulate their own questions?

Cognitive-offloading research predates AI and predicts the pattern: what you delegate, you deskill. Early workplace studies find generative AI reduces self-reported critical-thinking effort — the question is what practice regime prevents it.

What changed
March–August 2026 · swept August 3, 2026 · editorial review pending
01

The systematic evidence converged on a dual-mechanism answer: the same tool amplifies higher-order thinking under structured practice and substitutes for it under unguided use — so 'does AI deskill us' is the wrong question, and 'under what regime' is the right one. The homogenization worry got more precise too: AI collapses the structural form of writing sharply while leaving, or even widening, viewpoint diversity.

Fawzia Omer Alubthane · Frontiers in Psychology · 22 Jun 2026 paper
Amplifier or substitute? A systematic review of generative AI's impact on higher-order cognitive skills
GenAI functions as a cognitive amplifier under structured pedagogical conditions and as a cognitive substitute under unguided use.

A systematic review of 89 studies converging on a dual-mechanism conclusion: the same tool builds or bypasses higher-order thinking depending on the practice regime — reframing the deskilling question as one about conditions.

Inoshita, Omura, Yamanaka, Maeda & Tsuji · arXiv · 22 Mar 2026 paper
Does AI Homogenize Student Thinking? A Multi-Dimensional Analysis of Structural Convergence in AI-Augmented Essays
The effect is dimension-specific: cohesion architecture lost 70–78% of its variance, whereas perspective plurality was diversified.

Measures whether AI collapses the diversity of student writing and finds it selective, not uniform: structural form converges sharply while viewpoint diversity can widen — a more precise account of the homogenization worry.

Jain, Shajit, Maini, Shahin & Panda · Cognitive Processing · 30 Jun 2026 paper
Cognitive offloading, critical thinking and attitudes towards artificial intelligence in the era of ChatGPT
Although AI improves task accuracy and lowers mental effort, it does not necessarily save time or uniformly enhance or reduce critical thinking.

An experimental AI-assisted vs manual comparison that complicates the simple offloading-erodes-thinking story, finding accuracy and effort gains without a uniform critical-thinking cost.
