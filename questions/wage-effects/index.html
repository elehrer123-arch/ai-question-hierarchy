Wages

Could AI reduce worker bargaining power and wages even without producing large increases in unemployment?

Acemoglu-Restrepo show 'so-so automation' can depress wages without mass unemployment. Autor's counter-case: AI could rebuild middle-skill work by extending expertise to more people. Both are live.

What changed
January–August 2026 · swept August 3, 2026 · editorial review pending
01

The wage question separated from the labor-share question: Autor and Kausik show automation can raise wages while cutting labor share, and Autor reframed the risk as devaluation of expertise. On the ground, NY Fed postings data find no distinct AI-driven demand decline — while Smith documents the skill premium compressing inside software.

Autor & Kausik · MIT Economics · 13 Jan 2026 paper
Resolving the Automation Paradox: Falling Labor Share, Rising Wages
When labor share exceeds this wage-maximizing level, further automation increases wages even while reducing labor's output share.

Model plus twelve-country data: automation can raise wages while reducing labor's share — the two questions come apart.

David Autor · Issues in Science and Technology · 13 Jan 2026 interview
How Is AI Shaping the Future of Work?
The concern is about devaluation of expertise

The main risk is falling returns to accumulated expertise, not job scarcity; proposes wage insurance and universal basic capital.

Audoly, Guerin & Topa · NY Fed, Liberty Street Economics · 14 May 2026 post
Do Job Postings Show Early Labor-Market Effects of AI?
The evidence from job postings provides little indication of a distinct AI-driven decline in labor demand.

Lightcast postings against exposure scores: declines predate ChatGPT; no junior-senior divergence.

Noah Smith · Noahpinion · 5 Feb 2026 post
The Fall of the Nerds

AI coding tools compress the software skill premium the way power looms devalued master weavers — wages and status, not employment.

Tiffany Wilding · PIMCO · 21 May 2026 report
AI, Market Power, and Diminishing Labor Share

Asset-manager macro case that scalability plus hyperscaler market power push labor share down without large unemployment.

Zvi Mowshowitz · Don't Worry About the Vase · 14 Jan 2026 post
When Will They Take Our Jobs?

Argues comparative-advantage defenses of permanent human wages fail once humans become net-negative contributors; the collaboration era lasts a decade or two.
