4.1.4

Transparency

What should companies disclose about training, evaluations, incidents, energy use, safeguards, and deployment?

After the first Foundation Model Transparency Index publicly named laggards, measured disclosure rose — though training-data and compute details remain thin. Mandatory incident reporting — the aviation-safety model — is the most commonly proposed floor beneath voluntary disclosure.

What changed · October 2025–August 2026 · latest sweep August 3, 2026 · editorial review pending
01

Measured transparency went backwards: the Foundation Model Transparency Index fell 17 points against stiffened criteria, with training data the most opaque domain — reversing the index's earlier finding that naming laggards raised disclosure. A novel constitutional constraint arrived from the other direction: mandatory disclosure of trade secrets may trigger Fifth Amendment takings claims, an argument any disclosure regime now has to design around.

Micah Musser · Lawfare · 2026-06 essay
AI Regulation and the Looming Problem of the Takings Clause
Regulations that force developers to disclose trade secrets to the public could violate the Constitution.

Novel constitutional constraint on the transparency agenda: because trade secrets are protected property, mandatory public disclosure of training-data and model details could trigger Fifth Amendment takings claims.

Malihe Alikhani & Aidan T. Kane · Brookings · 2025-12 essay
What is California's AI safety law?
Each large developer must design, implement, and publicly post a company-wide safety and risk management plan describing how catastrophic risks are identified, assessed, and reduced.

The reference explainer for SB 53 implementation: how the first binding US frontier-transparency statute converts voluntary safety frameworks into enforceable publication, 15-day incident reporting, and whistleblower duties.
