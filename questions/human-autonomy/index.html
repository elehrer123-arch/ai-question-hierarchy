5.5.3

Human autonomy

How much control should humans retain if AI systems consistently make decisions that appear wiser, safer, or more benevolent?

Danaher's 'algocracy': rule by systems whose reasoning citizens cannot contest. The modern twist is that deference could be freely chosen, one convenient delegation at a time, and still end somewhere irreversible.

01

The autonomy question got separated from the takeover question. A co-author of Gradual Disempowerment argues that even faithfully aligned AI could make keeping humans in the decision loop look irresponsible, so competitive pressure erodes self-government without anyone seizing power — the value-level worry distinct from the control mechanism tracked under Safety. The philosophy literature catalogues how 'algorithmic paternalism' undermines the preconditions of self-determination even when the machine decides well.
