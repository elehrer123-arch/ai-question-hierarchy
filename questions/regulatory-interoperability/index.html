Interoperability

Can different national regulatory systems recognize common evaluations, audits, and safety standards?

Mutual recognition of evals and audits is the trade-friction question: without it, compliance fragments by jurisdiction; with it, the strictest regime quietly sets the global floor (or the loosest one erodes it).

What changed
January–August 2026 · swept August 3, 2026 · editorial review pending
01

Mutual recognition now has an institution-building effort behind it: Brundage's AVERI aims to make third-party frontier audits rigorous and universal enough for jurisdictions to recognize each other's assurance — against the warning that marketplace auditing recreates credit-rating-agency incentives and could institutionalize a false floor. China entered the standards contest too, proposing agent-interoperability and mutual-trust standards at WAIC.
