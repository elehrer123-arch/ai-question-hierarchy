4.3.1

Race dynamics

Does strategic competition encourage beneficial investment or cause firms and states to cut safety margins and deploy prematurely?

Race models formalize the intuition: the closer the competition, the less anyone spends on safety. Danzig's 'technology roulette' argument extends it — even the winner inherits systems it doesn't fully control.

What changed · May–August 2026 · latest sweep August 3, 2026 · editorial review pending
01

The race narrative itself came under scrutiny: a scholarly analysis of eight years of policy documents finds the AGI-race framing lacks evidentiary support on the Chinese side and functions as self-fulfilling prophecy, and reporting documents how labs instrumentalized China framing to win deregulation. The experimental result cuts deepest: falling behind — not individual risk appetite — drives unsafe development choices, implying structural fixes rather than actor-level norms. The constructive proposal is 'safety in parallel': each side hardening its own practices, with channels between them.
