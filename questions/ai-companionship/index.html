Companionship

Will AI companions reduce loneliness and provide support, or displace difficult but developmentally important human relationships?

One Harvard team's experiments found an AI companion reduced momentary loneliness about as much as talking to a person — a short-run result from one research group, not a settled finding. Whether companions displace the human relationships people would otherwise form has not been measured either way.

What changed
April–August 2026 · swept August 3, 2026 · editorial review pending
01

The evidence turned less favorable this spring: longitudinal data linking chatbot use to increased loneliness, a first national youth census finding frequent users lonelier, and developmental work naming the mechanism — relationships not formed. The short-run-benefit result now has serious company. The retirement of GPT-4o and the #keep4o backlash turned model attachment into a mass phenomenon rather than an edge case (tracked in depth under engineered dependency, 5.3.2).

First annual national census of youth AI use; links frequent use to loneliness and to feeling more understood by AI than by people.

Ha, Figueroa, McGray et al. (ASU) · ASU News / The Lancet Child & Adolescent Health · 29 Jun 2026 report
AI companionship poses risks for teen development
If you're given full satisfaction on everything, you don't have learning experience with challenges or obstacles.

Developmental-psychology work naming relational displacement as the mechanism by which frictionless companionship could stunt relationship skills.

Folk & Dunn · Psychological Science · Apr 2026 paper
How Does Turning to AI for Companionship Predict Loneliness and Vice Versa?
Increased social chatbot use predicted increased loneliness, using a single-item measure of emotional isolation.

Longitudinal evidence for a bidirectional loop: lonely people turn to chatbots, and chatbot use then predicts more loneliness.
