5.2.2

Learning versus outsourcing

When does AI tutoring deepen understanding, and when does it allow people to bypass the work that creates understanding?

The cautionary RCT: unrestricted GPT access improved homework performance and worsened exam performance — help that substitutes for effort creates 'cognitive debt' rather than learning.

What changed · December 2025–August 2026 · latest sweep August 3, 2026 · editorial review pending
01

The evidence turned genuinely two-sided. Observational work found the exact substitution the question fears — AI cut study time on math problems and the proctored retention that effortful practice builds, dropping about 25% over eleven quarters — and the viral 'cognitive debt' EEG study drew a serious peer rebuttal on sample size and methods. But a controlled experiment found unrestricted AI tutoring beat restricted access without crowding out reading, so the split is real: outcome depends on whether the tool sequences difficulty and demands effort or spoonfeeds solutions.

Rismanchian, Uzun, Matayoshi, Cosyn & Kurd-Misto · arXiv · 2026-05 paper
Faster Completion, Less Learning: Generative AI Reduced Study Time on Math Problems and the Knowledge They Build
Learning time on AI-susceptible problems declines 2.8% per quarter among college students after ChatGPT's release, cumulating to 26.9% over eleven quarters.

Large-scale observational evidence of exactly the substitution the question is about: proctored retention drops ~25% while non-proctored performance rises — AI raising measured output while eroding the effortful practice that builds knowledge.

Mira Fischer, Holger A. Rau & Rainer Michael Rilke · IZA Institute of Labor Economics · 2025-12 paper
AI Tutoring Enhances Student Learning Without Crowding Out Reading Effort
unrestricted access significantly outperforms restricted access by 0.21 standard deviations, contradicting concerns about premature AI reliance.

An experimental counterpoint: unrestricted AI access beats restricted access and does not crowd out textbook reading, with gains concentrating among lower-baseline students who self-regulate — the constructive side of the split.

Stankovic, Hirche, Kollatzsch & Doetsch · arXiv · 2025-12 paper
Comment on: Your Brain on ChatGPT: Accumulation of Cognitive Debt
Our primary concerns focus on: (i) study design considerations, including the limited sample size; (ii) the reproducibility of the analyses; (iii) methodological issues related to the EEG analysis

The serious peer rebuttal to the viral MIT 'cognitive debt' EEG study, cataloguing sample-size, reproducibility, and EEG-analysis problems — the reference a reader tracking the cognitive-debt claim needs alongside the original.

Jill Barshay · The Hechinger Report · 2026-04 news
The quest to build a better AI tutor
Some studies have found that chatbot tutors can backfire because students lean on them too heavily, get spoonfed solutions and fail to absorb the material.

Reports a UPenn RCT with ~800 students showing that how a tutor sequences difficulty determines whether it deepens learning or spoonfeeds — the design question at the heart of learning-vs-outsourcing.
