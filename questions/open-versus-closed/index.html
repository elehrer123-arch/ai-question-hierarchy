Open versus closed models

When does openness improve competition, research, and accountability, and when does it irreversibly proliferate dangerous capability?

The marginal-risk framing asks what open weights add beyond existing tools; the irreversibility framing answers that releases can't be recalled when capabilities cross dangerous thresholds. Both are right about different regimes.

Our position on open-weights models
Open-weights models that don't have dangerous capabilities are a public good: they don't cost anything besides the compute needed to run them.

Anthropic's CEO formally disavows any ban on open weights and reframes the lab's position around chip export controls, anti-distillation enforcement, and capability-based safety testing that applies to open and closed models alike — a significant repositioning in the 2026 fight.
