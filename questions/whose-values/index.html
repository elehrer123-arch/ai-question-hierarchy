4.5.8

Whose values

When AI systems must encode contested values, who should decide what they refuse, favor, and optimize for — users, developers, governments, or some collective process?

The political half of alignment: 2.1.1 asks whether values can be specified at all; this question asks who legitimately sets them. The operating answer is lab-authored model specs and constitutions, with experiments in public input so far small and one-off — and political philosophy has begun treating that arrangement as a question of governing power rather than product design.

What changed · October 2025–August 2026 · latest sweep August 3, 2026 · editorial review pending
01

The 'who decides' question acquired an answer nobody proposed: procurement. OMB's implementation of the woke-AI order makes 'ideological neutrality' a binding criterion for federal LLM purchases, comparative research finds government AI laws increasingly setting what chatbots refuse across jurisdictions, and the constructive proposals — public-reasoning-fidelity frameworks for government-procured models — try to give the public a say in values chosen on its behalf.
