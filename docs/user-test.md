# Reader test protocol

Five readers, ~25 minutes each, run separately. The goal is not praise or a
feature wishlist — it is to find where the site fails to communicate what it
is and what it is for.

## Recruiting

Aim for a spread: two people who follow AI closely, two who are generally
informed but not immersed, one who is skeptical of AI discourse altogether.
Avoid people who have already seen the site.

## Setup

Send only the URL — no explanation, no framing. Ask them to share their
screen and **think aloud**: say what they are looking at, what they expect a
click to do, and when something confuses them. Record if they consent.

Say once, at the start: "I did not build this to be defended. Confusion is
the useful result, so please narrate it rather than working around it."

## Script

**1. First 60 seconds (do not intervene).**
Let them land and look. Then ask, before they scroll further:
- What is this?
- Who do you think made it, and why?
- What would you use it for, if anything?

**2. Orientation.**
- Where would you look to find out how current this is?
- Is anything on this page written by a person? Which parts?
  *(This is the trust test — it targets the sweep/review distinction.)*

**3. Directed task.** Pick one they'd plausibly care about:
- "Find out what's happening with AI and entry-level jobs."
- "Find out whether anyone thinks AI could be conscious."
Watch which surface they choose (Map, Browse, Latest, search) and where
they stall. Do not help until they ask twice.

**4. The stream.**
Send them to `/latest/`.
- What is this list? Where do these come from?
- Would you come back to it? At what interval?
- What does "6 featured from 8 substantive pieces tracked" mean to you?
  *(If they read it as "only 8 things exist online," the wording has failed.)*

**5. Trust probe.**
Open any question page and point at "latest sweep … · editorial review
pending."
- What does that tell you?
- Does it make you trust the page more or less?
- What would you want to be true before citing this page in an argument?

**6. Close.**
- If this disappeared tomorrow, would you miss it? What specifically?
- Who else would you send it to — and what would you say in the message?
  *(The second half is the real answer. If they can't write the sentence,
  the site's proposition isn't legible yet.)*

## What to record

For each reader, one line per moment of: hesitation, wrong expectation
(clicked X expecting Y), re-reading, and any sentence they use to describe
the site in their own words. Those sentences are the most valuable output —
they are candidate copy.

## Reading the results

- **Same confusion in 3+ readers** → fix the site, not the explanation.
- **Nobody uses Browse** → it may be scaffolding rather than a surface.
- **Nobody notices the review status** → the trust work isn't landing where
  readers look.
- **Everyone describes it differently** → the homepage is not stating the
  proposition plainly enough.

Do not fix anything during the sessions, and do not run the fixes past the
same five people afterward; recruit fresh readers for a second round.
